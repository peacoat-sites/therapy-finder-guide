Anxiety is the most common mental health challenge in the United States, affecting tens of millions of adults each year. Yet anxiety is frequently misunderstood, minimized, or simply left unaddressed. People often assume that worry and nervousness are just personality traits rather than symptoms that can be assessed and treated. The GAD-7 (Generalized Anxiety Disorder 7-item scale) gives you a structured, clinically validated way to measure how much anxiety may be affecting you.

## About the GAD-7 Screening Questionnaire

The GAD-7 was developed by Dr. Robert Spitzer and colleagues and published in 2006 in the Archives of Internal Medicine. Like the PHQ-9 for depression, it was designed to help busy primary care clinicians quickly identify patients who may benefit from further evaluation and treatment. The questionnaire is now used throughout the world in clinical settings, research studies, and public health programs.

The seven questions in the GAD-7 assess the core symptoms of generalized anxiety disorder: excessive worrying, difficulty controlling worry, restlessness, fatigue, difficulty concentrating, irritability, and physical tension. Respondents rate how often they have experienced each symptom over the past two weeks. Scores range from 0 to 21, with established thresholds for mild (5-9), moderate (10-14), and severe (15-21) anxiety.

The GAD-7 has high sensitivity and specificity for generalized anxiety disorder, and research has also validated its use for detecting panic disorder, social anxiety disorder, and post-traumatic stress disorder. However, like all screening tools, it is not a diagnostic instrument. Diagnosis requires a comprehensive clinical evaluation.

## What Your Score Means

The GAD-7 score gives you and your healthcare provider a shared, objective language for discussing anxiety symptoms. Here is a general guide to what different score ranges suggest:

**Minimal (0-4):** Low symptom burden. Everyday stress and worry are not unusual, but at this level they are unlikely to be impairing your functioning in significant ways. Check in with yourself periodically, and consider some [mindfulness or relaxation strategies](/cognitive-behavioral-therapy-techniques-for-anxiety/) if stress feels elevated.

**Mild (5-9):** Some anxiety symptoms present, but possibly manageable with self-care, lifestyle changes, and social support. A brief check-in with your primary care provider is reasonable if symptoms persist or worsen.

**Moderate (10-14):** This is the threshold where clinical guidelines typically recommend discussing treatment with a provider. Effective, evidence-based treatments exist for anxiety disorders, including cognitive-behavioral therapy (CBT), which has a strong track record.

**Severe (15-21):** Significant anxiety symptoms that are likely causing meaningful interference with daily life, relationships, or work. Professional evaluation is strongly recommended. Please reach out to a mental health professional as soon as you are able to. If your anxiety is accompanied by thoughts of self-harm or you are in crisis, contact the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline immediately.

## What Comes Next

If your GAD-7 score suggests moderate or severe anxiety, the most important next step is talking to a professional who can conduct a thorough clinical evaluation and recommend treatment options. Effective treatments for anxiety include cognitive-behavioral therapy, acceptance and commitment therapy, exposure therapy, medication, and combinations of these approaches.

Our [guide to finding a therapist](/find-therapist/) can help you understand your options, what to expect in a first appointment, and how to navigate the practical challenges of starting therapy, including cost and insurance. You do not need a referral to see most therapists, and many offer telehealth appointments for added convenience.

Anxiety is highly treatable. Tens of millions of people have found significant relief through therapy, and you deserve the same opportunity. Your GAD-7 score is a starting point for a conversation, not a ceiling on your well-being.
