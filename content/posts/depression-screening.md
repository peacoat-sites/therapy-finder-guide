Depression is one of the most common mental health conditions in the world, yet millions of people experience weeks or months of persistent low mood, fatigue, or loss of interest without realizing it's something treatable. They don't have a name for what's happening. They don't know help exists. The PHQ-9 was built to fix exactly that.

## What Is the PHQ-9?

Drs. Robert Spitzer, Janet Williams, and Kurt Kroenke developed the PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9) as a fast, reliable way for primary care doctors to spot depression and measure how bad it is. Since its launch, hundreds of clinical studies have validated it across languages and populations worldwide. You'll find it in doctor's offices, therapist practices, research labs, and public health clinics.

The nine questions map directly to the DSM criteria for major depressive disorder. You rate each symptom from "not at all" to "nearly every day" over the past two weeks. The total score lands between 0 and 27, with specific thresholds for minimal, mild, moderate, moderately severe, and severe depression.

**Important:** This is a screening tool, not a diagnosis. A high score doesn't mean you definitely have clinical depression, and a low score doesn't rule it out. Only a licensed professional can diagnose you. What the PHQ-9 does is give you and your doctor real data to build a conversation on.

## Understanding Your PHQ-9 Score

Your score is a conversation starter, not a final answer. Here's what each range typically means:

**Minimal (0-4):** Few or no depressive symptoms right now. Keep an eye on yourself. Reach out if things shift.

**Mild (5-9):** Mild distress or situational stress showing up. Self-care, watchful waiting, and a check-in with your doctor usually work here.

**Moderate (10-14):** This is where clinical guidelines say active treatment helps. Therapy, medication, or both. [Finding a therapist](/find-therapist/) is a solid first move.

**Moderately severe (15-19) or severe (20-27):** You're dealing with significant distress. Professional evaluation and treatment aren't optional. If you're already working with someone, show them these results. If not, reach out to a mental health professional soon.

## When to Seek Help Right Away

Thoughts of suicide or self-harm don't wait for a score. Call or text 988 immediately. Text HOME to 741741 for the Crisis Text Line. Go to your nearest ER. Depression responds to treatment, and crisis lines exist because these moments happen.

If your results point toward professional support, you have more paths than you probably think. Many therapists charge on a sliding scale. Telehealth lets you work from home.
