It took me two years and three false starts to find a therapist who fit, so I can tell you the two questions that decide most searches before personality even enters into it: what does a session cost where you live, and is the person you found actually allowed to see you there? The second one surprises people. Teletherapy feels borderless, but a video session legally happens in the state where the client is sitting, and every state licenses clinicians separately.

There is one big exception to that patchwork: PSYPACT, the interstate compact that lets psychologists practice by video across member states. As of July 2026 it covers 40 states plus DC, with Alaska and Iowa signed on and joining. ## How to read the compact column

PSYPACT covers doctoral-level psychologists who hold a special authority (the E.Passport plus APIT credential). If your state is a member, a PSYPACT psychologist sitting in any other member state can legally treat you by video. Two situations where this changes everything:

- **You move.** From Georgia to Michigan, both members: you keep your psychologist. From Georgia to California: the relationship ends unless your psychologist happens to hold a California license, which most out-of-state clinicians do not.
- **You live somewhere with a thin local market.** In a rural member state, PSYPACT opens the door to thousands of psychologists in the other 40 member jurisdictions, often with shorter waitlists and lower rates than your nearest city.

The eight holdout states are not small ones: California, New York, Massachusetts, Oregon, Hawaii, Louisiana, Montana, and New Mexico. If you live in one of them, restrict your search to clinicians licensed in your state. Every legitimate directory lets you filter by state licensure, and you can verify any license number free on your state board's website.

One caution in the other direction: PSYPACT is for psychologists only. Most working therapists in the US are master's-level clinicians (LPCs, LMFTs, LCSWs), and their compacts are younger. The Counseling Compact has been enacted in over 35 states and is beginning to issue privileges; the Social Work Licensure Compact is enacted in about 30 states but not yet issuing. If a counselor tells you they can see you across state lines today, ask which authority that rests on before you book.

## What the cost column really tells you

The ranges are typical self-pay rates for a standard 45 to 60 minute individual session. They are wide on purpose: within one state, a rural counselor and a downtown psychologist can be $100 apart. A few patterns worth knowing:

- **The coasts carry a premium that compounds.** New York and California sessions commonly run $150 to $300. At weekly frequency that is $7,800 to $15,600 a year, which is why the [FSA and HSA route](/fsa-hsa-for-therapy-costs/) matters more in those states than anywhere else.
- **Low-cost states are often low-supply states.** Mississippi and West Virginia have some of the country's lowest session rates and also some of its worst therapist-to-population ratios. PSYPACT membership helps both: all three of the cheapest states in the table are members, so residents can also shop the wider compact market.
- **Listed price is the ceiling, not the floor.** Roughly half of private-practice therapists offer sliding-scale spots. Our guide to [free and low-cost therapy options](/free-or-low-cost-therapy-options/) covers how to ask, and training clinics routinely run $30 to $60 in even the priciest metros.

If you want to translate a range into your actual annual number for weekly, biweekly, or monthly sessions, the [therapy cost calculator](/therapy-cost-calculator/) does that math, and the full breakdown of what drives pricing is in [how much therapy costs](/how-much-does-therapy-cost/).

## Deciding between local and cross-state care

Having done this search both ways, my honest take: if your state is in the compact and you live outside a major metro, searching the whole compact usually finds a better fit faster, and often cheaper, because you are no longer limited to whoever has openings within driving distance. The tradeoffs are real, though. Crisis support is harder when your clinician is three states away, some issues genuinely benefit from in-person work, and [video versus in-person](/online-therapy-vs-in-person/) is not a tie for everyone.

Rules move quickly in this space. Two states joined the compact in the two months before this page was written, and the counselor and social worker compacts will change the picture for master's-level therapists over the next couple of years. I update this table when a state's status changes.
