On November 9, 2026, Compass Pathways submitted a New Drug Application to the FDA for COMP360, a synthesized psilocybin therapy for treatment-resistant depression. That single filing made history. COMP360 is now the first classic psychedelic ever to reach this stage of U.S. regulatory review, and the timeline is moving faster than most people outside the clinical world realize.

The NDA is backed by data from two Phase 3 trials and a Phase 2b study that together enrolled more than 1,000 participants, all of them living with highly chronic, treatment-resistant depression, according to the official November 9 news release filed with the SEC. The FDA awarded COMP360 a Priority Review Voucher in April 2026, which compresses the standard review target to six months from the standard twelve months. That means a decision could realistically land in early 2027. Compass is already targeting a commercial launch in the first half of that year, pending both FDA approval and DEA rescheduling of psilocybin, which is currently a Schedule I controlled substance. As Psychiatric Times reported on November 9, the drug also holds FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation and UK ILAP status, signals that regulators on both sides of the Atlantic have seen enough to accelerate their attention.

This matters far beyond the pharmaceutical industry. If COMP360 is approved, it won't work like a prescription you take home and fill at a pharmacy. The delivery model is fundamentally different from anything currently in mainstream [mental health care](/medicaid-cuts-are-coming-for-mental-health-care-now-what/), and patients, families, and clinicians are right to have questions.

## What "Treatment-Resistant Depression" Actually Means Here

Treatment-resistant depression, or TRD, is a clinical designation, not just a colloquial way of saying someone "hasn't found the right antidepressant yet." Most definitions require that a person has not responded adequately to at least two different antidepressant treatments of adequate dose and duration during a current episode. For some people in the COMP360 trials, the history of failed treatments was far longer than that.

This population matters because existing options are genuinely limited. Esketamine (Spravato) received FDA approval in 2019 for TRD and requires supervised in-office administration. ECT (electroconvulsive therapy) remains effective but carries stigma and tolerability concerns for many patients. For a subset of people with TRD, nothing currently approved has worked well. COMP360, if approved, would not replace those options. It would add one.

## How Psilocybin Therapy Actually Works, Clinically

The model Compass Pathways has studied is not recreational use. It is not a patient sitting alone in a clinic room. COMP360 therapy involves a carefully controlled setting, trained therapists present throughout the experience, and structured preparation and integration sessions before and after the psilocybin session itself.

That therapeutic support is not optional packaging. It's part of what's being studied. The drug and the clinical support are co-administered, which is a meaningful distinction from most FDA-approved medications. This has direct implications for how COMP360 might be delivered commercially, and why a standard pharmacy distribution model almost certainly won't apply.

A parallel trial, COMP202, is now studying COMP360 for PTSD under an FDA Investigational New Drug acceptance granted in January 2026. That's a Phase 2b/3 trial, still early relative to the TRD program, but it signals that the evidence base being built here is broader than a single condition.

## What Approval Would and Wouldn't Change Immediately

It's worth being precise about the timeline of change, because "FDA approval" and "available to patients" are not the same thing in this case.

| Step | What It Involves | Timing Estimate |
|---|---|---|
| FDA decision on NDA | Review of Phase 2b and Phase 3 trial data | Potentially early 2027 (NPV fast-track) |
| DEA rescheduling | Psilocybin must be moved from Schedule I | Months after FDA action; timeline uncertain |
| Certified facility buildout | Clinics must meet likely REMS-style requirements | Concurrent or post-DEA action |
| Therapist training and credentialing | Specialized training programs required | Ongoing; some already in development |
| Commercial availability | Patients can actually access treatment | First half of 2027 is Compass's target, but realistic access may lag |

The DEA rescheduling piece is not a formality. It's a separate federal process that has its own timeline and has caused delays in other drug approvals that required scheduling changes. Patients hoping to access COMP360 quickly after an FDA approval should understand that the gap between approval and actual availability could stretch meaningfully.

## What This Means If You or Someone You Know Has TRD

[Right now](/the-va-is-testing-mdma-assisted-therapy-for-ptsd-right-now/), today, COMP360 is not available outside of clinical trials. If you have treatment-resistant depression and are curious about psilocybin therapy, the most reliable path is checking ClinicalTrials.gov for currently enrolling studies. Your psychiatrist or prescribing clinician can help you assess whether you meet eligibility criteria for any active trials.

If you're currently in treatment for TRD, this news is not a reason to stop what's working, adjust your medications on your own, or delay other evidence-based care. Psilocybin therapy, if approved, will be one option among several. It's not positioned as a cure, and the research doesn't claim that.

For clinicians, particularly therapists and psychologists who may eventually be involved in COMP360-adjacent care, now is a reasonable time to look at professional training programs already developing in this space. Several universities and clinical training organizations have been building psilocybin therapy curricula in anticipation of exactly this regulatory moment.

## The Bigger Picture

What Compass Pathways did on November 9 is genuinely unprecedented in U.S. regulatory history for psychedelic medicine. That doesn't mean approval is guaranteed, and it doesn't mean the road from approval to patient access is short. But for the millions of people who have exhausted standard antidepressant options, this filing represents something real: a clinical program large enough and rigorous enough to put a psychedelic compound in front of the FDA's formal review process. The next few months will tell us a great deal about where it goes from here.

If you're managing depression or supporting someone who is, please talk with a licensed [mental health](/what-to-do-during-a-mental-health-crisis/) professional or psychiatrist about your specific situation. No article, however current, substitutes for that conversation.
