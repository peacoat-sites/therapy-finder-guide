Depression is one of the most common mental health conditions in the world, yet it is also one of the most under-recognized and under-treated. Many people experience weeks or months of persistent low mood, fatigue, or loss of interest without realizing that what they are going through has a name, and that effective help is available. The PHQ-9 (Patient Health Questionnaire-9) was developed precisely to close that gap.

## What Is the PHQ-9?

The PHQ-9 is a nine-question screening tool developed by Drs. Robert Spitzer, Janet Williams, and Kurt Kroenke and colleagues. It was designed to help primary care physicians quickly assess the presence and severity of depressive symptoms. The questionnaire has since been validated in hundreds of clinical studies across many languages and populations, and it is now used by mental health professionals, primary care doctors, researchers, and public health organizations worldwide.

Each of the nine items on the PHQ-9 maps directly to a diagnostic criterion for major depressive disorder from the DSM (Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders). Respondents rate how often they have experienced each symptom over the previous two weeks, from "not at all" to "nearly every day." Scores are summed to produce a total between 0 and 27, with established cut-points for minimal, mild, moderate, moderately severe, and severe symptom ranges.

**Important:** The PHQ-9 is a screening tool, not a diagnostic test. A high score does not mean you have clinical depression, and a low score does not rule it out. Only a licensed mental health professional or physician can provide a diagnosis. The tool helps you and your provider have a more informed conversation.

## How to Use This Screening Tool

## Understanding Your PHQ-9 Score

Depression screening results are a starting point for a conversation with a professional, not a final verdict. Here is how to think about each score range:

A score in the **minimal range (0-4)** suggests few or no depressive symptoms at this time. Continue monitoring your well-being and reach out if things change.

A score in the **mild range (5-9)** may reflect mild distress or situational stress. Watchful waiting and self-care strategies, combined with a check-in with your doctor, are often appropriate next steps.

A score in the **moderate range (10-14)** is when most clinical guidelines recommend active treatment. This might mean therapy, medication, or both, depending on your history and preferences. [Connecting with a therapist](/find-therapist/) is a good first step.

Scores in the **moderately severe (15-19) or severe (20-27) range** indicate significant distress. Professional evaluation and treatment are strongly recommended. If you are already seeing a provider, share these results with them. If you are not currently in care, please reach out to a mental health professional soon.

## When to Seek Help Right Away

If you are experiencing thoughts of suicide or self-harm, regardless of your total PHQ-9 score, please contact the 988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline immediately by calling or texting 988. You can also text HOME to 741741 to reach the Crisis Text Line, or go to your nearest emergency room. Depression is a medical condition, and crisis support exists for exactly these moments.

## Next Steps After Screening

If your score suggests you might benefit from professional support, you have more options than you might think. Many therapists offer sliding scale fees, and telehealth has made it easier than ever to access care from home.
