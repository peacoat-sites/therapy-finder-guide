Therapy apps are genuinely useful for some people and genuinely insufficient for others. That distinction matters more than any feature comparison, so let's start there: if you're managing a diagnosed condition like bipolar disorder, experiencing psychosis, or in acute crisis, an app is not a substitute for clinical care. Full stop. For everyone else, the right app can bridge gaps [between sessions](/your-therapist-knows-youre-using-ai-between-sessions/), build skills, or serve as a first step when in-person therapy feels too expensive or too far away.

The market has expanded dramatically. BetterHelp, Talkspace, Headspace, Calm, Woebot, and a dozen others compete for the same users, and their differences are significant enough that picking the wrong one wastes both money and motivation.

## The two categories most people conflate

There's a common misconception that all [therapy apps](/prescription-digital-therapeutics-what-fda-cleared-therapy-apps-mean-f/) provide therapy. Most don't. The category splits cleanly into two types, and confusing them leads to disappointment.

The first type connects users with actual licensed clinicians, via text, audio, or video. BetterHelp and Talkspace are the dominant players here. You're matched with a therapist, you communicate through the platform, and you pay a subscription that covers a certain number of sessions or messaging volume. This is real therapy, though the format is less traditional.

The second type is skill-building or psychoeducation software. Headspace, Calm, Woebot, Sanvello, and Wysa fall here. No licensed therapist is monitoring your progress. You're getting guided meditations, CBT-based exercises, mood tracking, or chatbot interactions. These tools have real value, but calling them "therapy" is a stretch. They're closer to a well-designed workbook than a clinical relationship.

## Choosing by situation, not by marketing

The honest decision rule: if you want to talk to a licensed therapist, use BetterHelp or Talkspace, then verify credentials in your state's licensing database. If you want to build a mindfulness practice or manage everyday stress, Headspace or Calm will serve you well. If you want structured CBT exercises without paying for therapy, Sanvello or Woebot are worth trying.

One thing the app comparison articles rarely mention: therapist quality on live [therapy platforms](/best-online-therapy-platforms/) varies considerably. BetterHelp has faced criticism about its matching algorithm and therapist vetting, and Talkspace has had its own controversies around therapist compensation affecting retention. Neither is a dealbreaker, but they're reasons to treat the first match as provisional. Both platforms let you switch therapists. Use that option if the fit is poor, ideally within the first two weeks.

If you need a broader search, [Psychology Today's therapist directory](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/therapists) lets you filter by insurance, specialty, and telehealth availability, and includes therapists who work outside any single platform.

## The insurance question is worth asking twice

Most employer health plans cover traditional therapy at some negotiated rate. Far fewer cover app subscriptions outright. That said, some plans have added Talkspace or similar platforms as in-network options, and Employee Assistance Programs (EAPs) sometimes provide a few free sessions through these platforms. The only reliable way to find out is to call the member services number on your insurance card and ask specifically: "Does my plan cover any telehealth therapy apps or platforms?" Don't assume the answer is no.

**Example:** Assume your plan covers telehealth at 80% after a $500 deductible, and Talkspace is in-network. At $350/month for the therapy subscription, your out-of-pocket after the deductible is met would be roughly $70/month. That's meaningfully different from paying full price. The math depends on your specific plan, so verify before assuming.

## When an app isn't enough

Apps don't prescribe medication. They don't diagnose. They can't legally provide evidence-based trauma treatment (like EMDR or CPT) in the same way a trained clinician can in a structured setting. If you've been using an app for three months and you're not noticing any improvement in mood, sleep, or functioning, that's a signal to escalate to in-person or synchronous video therapy with a licensed provider.

And if you're in crisis right now, please contact the [988 Suicide and Crisis Lifeline](https://988lifeline.org/) by calling or texting 988. No app is designed for that moment.

For readers who want to deepen their own skill work between sessions, CBT-based workbooks like *Mind Over Mood* by Greenberger and Padesky remain among the most evidence-supported self-guided resources available. (If you purchase through links on this site, we may earn a small commission.)
